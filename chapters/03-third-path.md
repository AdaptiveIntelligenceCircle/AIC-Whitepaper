## THIRD PATH  

The Third Path is not a new concept; it first appeared in political theories in the late 1990s. It was developed as a clever and logical option for weaker nations seeking stability without dependence on superpowers like Russia, the United States, or China. The idea of the Third Way was initially considered impractical according to market principles, as weaker nations would have to rely on larger nations for supply chain development and even security protection from attacks. This always has two sides: without cooperation, the nation could face pressure from larger nations, increasing the risk of large-scale war. Conversely, while cooperation, to some extent, might bring economic and security benefits, it could lead to control over supply chains, knowledge, legislation, and free trade. This is a harsh reality, and its root cause dates back thousands of years, stemming from one of the most fundamental human attributes: tribalism. Tribalism is not only a primitive social phenomenon, but also the fundamental logic governing international relations and modern economics. When large nations form “power tribes,” they establish the rules of the game, supply chains, and knowledge norms. Weaker nations, if they want to survive, are forced to choose sides or accept being drawn into their control. This is a global manifestation of tribal instinct. In the field of technology, especially AI, Big Tech operates like modern tribes. They control computing infrastructure, data, and research standards. Independent projects like AIC face pressure from these tribes: they are simultaneously recognized, scrutinized, and sometimes constrained to prevent them from escaping their orbit. This clearly reflects that tribalism exists not only in international politics but is also deeply ingrained in the global knowledge and technology structure. 

Essentially, the third path is the most challenging project I've ever undertaken compared to any previous one. Why? Because it requires the necessary sacrifices to create an unassailable advantage; in this case, AIC has had to confront major institutions since January 2026 and seek exploratory cooperation in various ways. This shows that the third path in AI is a unique and strategic direction, as it creates a pincer movement between the familiar two superpowers, the US and China. I must emphasize that this is a courageous decision for many objective reasons, but it is necessary to build trust from those who want to build the human infrastructure. AIC's perspective on infrastructure is very different from most other classic projects. The third path means ethical principles at its core, rejecting all investment from large capitalist enterprises, and not owning or selling any services for personal gain. To elaborate a bit, AIC is a protocol where the community can distribute but still contributes to the shared vision of AIC. Technically speaking, AIC has redefined the concept of the "third way" to a higher level. With AIC, the third way goes beyond policy of common OSS; it has become a vital mechanism of the Human Meaning Network. We recognize that people are a crucial infrastructure alongside software and hardware, as traditional computer science theory suggests, and developing people alongside technology in a responsible and humane way strengthens the practicality of the third way. In any cyberattack, the most important factor is sometimes not the software or servers, but the people. Therefore, AIC has inherited from its predecessors but also created a different vision from those who came before it. It is the culmination of Richard Stallmann's philosophy of software freedom, kernel-centric approaches like Linus Torvalds, Vitalik Buterin's decentralized networks, Elon Musk's ecosystem expansion and Nguyen Duc Tri's entire contemplative observation, all aimed at restructuring civilizational infrastructure ethically and creating meaning. This is an ambitious goal, a long-term mission that I and the future community are striving towards.
