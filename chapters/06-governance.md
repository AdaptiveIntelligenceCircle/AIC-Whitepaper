# GOVERNANCE MODEL 

We begin with the Governance Model. This is the horizontal layer within the entire AIC ecosystem, as discussed in Part III. It's also the most crucial layer, not only for preserving the Ethical Kernel but also as a shield against internal conflicts and external pressure from major institutions. To survive in today's complex world, concepts like Governance have become extremely necessary, especially those with the potential to influence neutral and global areas. Typically, most projects only focus on Governance once they reach a certain user base, but with a civilized restructuring protocol, Governance becomes a vital goal and strategy, if not the most important, within the entire AIC ecosystem. The Governance Model aims for transparency, accountability, and risk management. In an ethical protocol, failing to place Governance at the final level (horizontal layer) would have disastrous consequences. We've seen several OSS projects fail or be manipulated due to inconsistencies in governance, and AIC is preventing this scenario. Governance is placed at the lower layer, meaning it reinforces and protects the entire Ethical Kernel from being derailed, allowing the upper layers to function stably.

One thing I discovered during the development of AIC is that governance can become a vital mechanism for the entire ecosystem. Besides providing the usual policies and legal commitments, governance also acts as a security layer against information manipulation or user data theft. When the distributed infrastructure operates on a large scale, it clashes with the community and human nature; in that case, governance is the only evidence of transparency and trustworthiness. I will delve into this aspect of governance with the following structure:

•	The Founder retains strategic veto power on core direction as long as he is able and willing.

•	Succession is not a transfer of ownership, but a distributed stewardship model.

•	Any successor or committee must uphold zero-donation and Third Path Absolute without exception.

•	Emergency succession and planned succession are handled differently.

In case the Founder becomes unavailable (health, military service, force majeure), an Emergency Steering Group of 2–3 Trusted Admins (already designated) will temporarily maintain the project.

•	They can only perform:
    o	Security patches
    o	Bug fixes
    o	Governance maintenance
    o	Public communication (with transparency)

•	They cannot change licenses, accept funding with strings, or alter ethical kernel direction.

And after reading the above structure, you should have a basic understanding of the Governance Model. Of course, AIC's Governance also has another characteristic: it is inheritable by subsequent generations who carry on the same third-path ideology as AIC. And anyone involved in building the AIC network must adhere to it. The Technical Steering Committee is responsible for day-to-day technical direction and governance of AIC/HMN while preserving the Founder's strategic oversight and the project's core principles. The responsibilities of contributors are very clear: 

•	Define and maintain technical roadmap for individual modules.
•	Review and merge critical PRs.
•	Issue triage and prioritization.
•	Propose improvements to architecture and governance.
•	Represent the project in technical discussions.

In addition, AIC has another binding clause, the NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement). This clause is implemented as follows:
+ Applies only to governance discussions and financial information managed through Open Collective (OCE).
+ It does not apply to code contributions, repositories, or any open-source materials licensed under GPL-3.0 and Open MDW 2025. Contributors agree not to disclose, share, or publish confidential governance or financial information without explicit permission from the Founder Admin.
+ Any breach of confidentiality may result in removal from the contributor group and revocation of privileges. This NDA remains in effect during the contributor’s involvement with AIC and for 12 months after departure. 

Furthermore, AIC's Security Layer doesn't just focus on conventional technical security (encryption, access control, DDoS protection), but prioritizes protecting ethical integrity and system meaning – this is a fundamental difference from current protocols.
